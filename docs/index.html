Where do you ablate to stop a rotor?

Machine learning and biophysics simulation applied to cardiac ablation targeting.

Atrial fibrillation is often sustained by rotors — rotating electrical waves in the heart wall. Ablation tries to stop them by destroying tissue. This project works through the open question of where, in four phases — and finds that the obvious target (the rotor's pivot point) is the wrong one.

The four phases

Phase 1ECG classification. A 1-D CNN on real 12-lead PTB-XL recordings — the signal-analysis baseline. Macro-F1 0.61, honestly reported.
Phase 2Rotor simulation + ground truth. Induce a reentrant rotor in openCARP, track its core by phase-singularity detection, train an electrogram classifier for core-adjacency.
Phase 3Simulate the ablation. Drop lesions at candidate sites, measure what terminates the rotor. Finding: ablate the nearby functional core, not the phase singularity.
Phase 4Real-time surrogate. A gradient-boosted model predicts the functional core from a 600 ms electrogram window — 1.8 mm median error, ~1600× faster than the simulation.
